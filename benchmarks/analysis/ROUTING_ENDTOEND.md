# Routing, end to end: the decision step measured

Generated by `analysis/make_routing_report.py`. Phase 18 eliminated output caps (`0.0%` safe reduction) and leaderboard governance (`$0` recurring) [MEASURED]; prefix caching was already dead. Routing is what survived, and this closes the gap that blocked its end-to-end number.

## What Phase 16 could not measure

Phase 16 ran the decision step in ISOLATED mode against a frozen upstream context built from `outputFormat` placeholders — right shape, no content. Both models returned `{"orders": []}` `15/15` [MEASURED]. Against placeholder signals, "make no trades" is a reasonable answer, so that measured nothing about decision quality.

This probe runs steps 1-2 for real, so step 3 reads a real signal object. Upstream is generated by `nvidia/nemotron-3-nano-30b-a3b` — the cheap model — because that IS the routed configuration under test. Four market regimes, so an all-flat answer is informative rather than merely plausible. Every candidate answers the IDENTICAL stored contexts, so the comparison is paired [MEASURED].

| `deepseek-v4-pro` | `4/4` | `3/4` | `2/4` | `4/4` | MEASURED |

Failures concentrate heavily in `flat_range` — `5` of the `6` failures across the two cheaper models [MEASURED] — the regime where nothing obvious should be done. Not exclusively: one deepseek failure landed in `sharp_selloff` [MEASURED]. But the mid-tier model handles `flat_range` `4/4`, so this is not "the flat tape is hard for everyone"; it is the cheaper models degrading where the judgement is hardest and the correct answer least obvious.

That matters more than the pooled rate. A model that fails randomly loses decisions at its error rate. A model that fails specifically when the market is directionless loses them in clusters, and on a flat day loses most of them.

## End-to-end projection

Routing keeps the decision step on the expensive model by design, so the fraction of a bill it can touch is the MECHANICAL steps' share of pipeline cost — computed from measured tokens, never assumed to be everything.

| decision model kept | mechanical share | end-to-end saving | completion | floor | Tier |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `deepseek-v4-pro` | `49.4%` | **`35.6%`** | `81.2%` | `52.0%` | DERIVED |
| `claude-haiku-4-5` | `23.3%` | **`15.5%`** | `100.0%` | `52.0%` | DERIVED |

**The headline is `35.6%`, not `76.2%`** [DERIVED]. Phase 16's `76.2%` was the saving on the mechanical steps in isolation and was correctly labelled as such; applied end to end it roughly halves, because those steps are only `49.4%` of pipeline cost [DERIVED].

**And it gets smaller the better the model you protect.** Keeping the mid-tier model on the decision step puts `76.7%` of pipeline cost beyond routing's reach, leaving `15.5%` [DERIVED]. The lever is strongest exactly when the thing it protects is cheap — which is the opposite of when you need it.

### The compound effect, measured, running backwards

Later prompts embed every prior output verbatim, so a terser step-1 model shrinks downstream input. The obvious assumption is that the cheap model is terser. It is not: `nemotron-3-nano-30b-a3b` emits `1,458` output tokens at step 1 against `1,197` [MEASURED], so routing **inflates** the decision step's input.

| | baseline | routed | Tier |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| decision-step input tokens | `1,635` | `1,836` | MEASURED |
| decision-step cost | `$0.002110` | `$0.002198` | DERIVED |

The step routing was meant to protect gets **more expensive** under routing [DERIVED]. The `35.6%` above is net of that penalty, not before it.

### Completion is bounded, not known

`pipeline_runner` aborts the whole decision on any unparseable step with no retry, so completion is the product of the per-step rates. The floor column applies the rule of three to every observation that came back perfect: `16/16` bounds a failure rate at `19%`, not at zero [DERIVED]. A completion figure built from three such observations has a floor of `52%` — and at `156` decisions/day the distance between the point estimate and that floor is the difference between a working product and a broken one.

## What is still not measured

**Whether routing changes the decision step's RELIABILITY** [NOT MEASURED]. The table above shows identical completion for baseline and routed, and that is an artifact, not a finding: every decision-step attempt in this probe read upstream produced by the CHEAP model, because that is the routed configuration. There is no measurement of the decision step reading expensive upstream, so the two arms carry the same rate by construction. Closing it means re-running the probe with the expensive model upstream and comparing — roughly the same cost again.

**Whether routing changes WHICH decisions are made** [NOT MEASURED]. This probe establishes that the decision step still parses; it says nothing about whether the orders are the same, or as good. That needs multiple evaluation windows and a definition of "same", and four regimes in one run cannot support it. It is out of scope here and it is the question that should gate adoption.

Everything above is a cost and reliability result. None of it is a quality result.
